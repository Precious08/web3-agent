## **Product Requirements Document**

---

# **1\. Product Overview**

## **1.1 Product Name**

**Web3 Research & Alpha Discovery Agent**

## **1.2 Product Summary**

A research and intelligence product for **Web3 alpha hunters, researchers, and analysts**.

The product helps users research:

* Projects  
* Tokens  
* Protocols  
* Ecosystems  
* Narratives  
* People  
* Market activity  
* On-chain activity  
* Social activity  
* Development activity  
* Funding  
* Emerging trends

The product goes beyond search and news aggregation.

Its purpose is to help users:

**Discover information → Understand it → Connect the dots → Identify meaningful signals → Validate them → Monitor what matters**

The product should reduce the time users spend manually searching across fragmented Web3 information while improving their ability to discover and investigate potentially valuable opportunities.

# **2\. Product Brief**

## **2.1 User**

**Alpha Hunters**

**Web3 Researchers**

**Web3 Analysts / Content Researchers**

### **Core User Need**

> "There is too much happening in Web3. I need to know what matters, why it matters, and what I should investigate next."

# **3\. Problem**

Web3 information is fragmented across many different sources and platforms.

A user researching a single project may need to search through social discussions, project websites, research articles, market information, ecosystem activity, development updates, funding announcements, community activity, and on-chain information.

This creates three primary problems.

### **3.1 Information Overload**

There is more Web3 information being produced than an individual researcher can realistically process.

### **3.2 Research Takes Too Much Time**

Users spend significant time moving between different sources, verifying information, comparing findings, and trying to understand context.

### **3.3 Valuable Signals Are Buried in Noise**

Potentially important developments can be hidden among:

* Hype  
* Speculation  
* Promotional content  
* Repetitive information  
* Short-lived trends  
* Unverified claims  
* Irrelevant activity

The product should help users separate meaningful signals from noise.

---

# **4\. Product Goal**

The product should make Web3 research:

**Faster, deeper, more contextual, and more useful for discovering early signals.**

The product should not attempt to guarantee profitable opportunities.

Instead, it should help users become better at:

* Finding relevant information  
* Understanding context  
* Connecting related information  
* Identifying meaningful changes  
* Evaluating evidence  
* Challenging assumptions  
* Monitoring important developments

# **6\. Product Principles**

## **6.1 Research Before Alpha**

The product should not immediately label something as alpha.

It should establish:

**What happened → Why it matters → What evidence supports it → What could contradict it**

## **6.2 Evidence Over Hype**

Important claims should be supported by relevant evidence.

## **6.3 Multiple Signals Beat Isolated Signals**

A single unusual event should rarely qualify as a strong signal.

The product should look for **signal convergence**.

## **6.4 Early Does Not Mean Valuable**

An early project or trend can still be weak.

Early discovery should be evaluated alongside evidence quality, significance, uniqueness, and potential impact.

## **6.5 Investigate Both Sides**

The product should actively search for information that supports and contradicts a potential thesis.

## **6.6 Explain the Reasoning**

Users should understand why something was surfaced.

## **6.7 User Makes the Final Judgment**

The product supports research and decision-making. It should not present speculative conclusions as guaranteed outcomes.

# **15\. Signal Assessment**

The product should evaluate signals using seven factors:

1. **Evidence quality**  
2. **Signal convergence**  
3. **Potential impact**  
4. **Magnitude of change**  
5. **Early-stage potential**  
6. **Uniqueness**  
7. **User relevance**

### **Signal assessment structure**

**Signal Strength**

Strong / Moderate / Weak / Uncertain

**Why it matters**

Explanation of the detected pattern.

**Supporting evidence**

Relevant evidence.

**Counter-signals**

Information that weakens the thesis.

**What remains unknown**

Unverified or incomplete information.

**What to investigate next**

Recommended research paths.

The product should present an assessment rather than a guaranteed prediction.

# **23\. Research Quality Framework**

The product should distinguish between:

### **Fact**

What is directly observed or supported.

### **Interpretation**

What the information may mean.

### **Signal**

Why the information may be meaningful.

### **Hypothesis**

What could happen if the trend continues.

### **Uncertainty**

What remains unknown.

This separation is essential for preventing speculation from being presented as fact.

# **43\. User Control Center — Backend / Settings Dashboard**

## **43.1 Purpose**

Every user must be able to control **what they see, why they see it, and how the agent reasons for them.**

The User Control Center is the backend/dashboard where users configure the entire product to match their research style, risk appetite, interests, and workflow.

It directly supports the core promise:

> "There is too much happening in Web3. I need to know what matters, why it matters, and what I should investigate next — **for me**."

No setting should be a black box. Every major personalization must be viewable, changeable, and resettable.

## **43.2 Design Principles for Settings**

1. **User Owns the Algorithm:** Any input that affects For You, Alpha Feed, Emerging Radar, Alerts, or Signal Assessment must be editable here.
2. **Explain Every Default:** Each setting shows what it does and what discovery the user will gain/lose.
3. **Progressive Control:** Basic mode for new users (Ecosystems, Narratives, Project Types), Advanced mode for power users (signal weights, thresholds, counter-signal tolerance).
4. **No Closed Bubble:** If filters are too narrow, the product warns and suggests Adjacent Discoveries.
5. **Instant Effect:** Changing a setting immediately re-tunes Home Dashboard, Feed, Radar, Search ranking, and Alerts.
6. **Resettable:** One-click "Reset to Defaults" per section + global reset.

## **43.3 Where It Lives**

* Accessible from: Home Dashboard header (gear icon), sidebar → Settings, and inline "Why am I seeing this? → Tune preferences" links on every Feed / Radar / Signal card.
* Structure: Single dashboard with left-nav sections (43.4 – 43.12 below).
* Onboarding: First-run wizard populates the most important settings (ecosystems, narratives, project types, experience level). User can skip and edit later.

* **Audit / Security Minimum:** Audited only / Audit preferred / No requirement
* **User's Role:** Airdrop hunter / Long-term investor / Developer / Researcher / Content creator — changes what "relevant" means

Each control supports: **Must-have / Interested / Muted / Blocked.**

Example:

> "Show me: Early-stage DePIN + AI x Crypto on Solana / Base, pre-token or points program, with dev activity, mute memecoins, block unaudited."

## **44.2 Narrative & Ecosystem Preferences**

* Followed Narratives (e.g. Restaking, Modular, RWA)
* Muted Narratives
* Followed Ecosystems + weight (e.g. Solana: High, Base: Medium)
* Emerging Tech Interests (ZK, FHE, Intent-based, etc.)
* "Surprise Me" slider: 0% (only my interests) → 100% (max adjacent discoveries). Default 25% to avoid bubble per Section 18.

## **44.3 People & Source Preferences**

* Preferred source types: Official docs / GitHub / On-chain / Research articles / Governance forums / Social
* Trusted vs. muted KOLs / analysts / publications
* Team background preferences: e.g. "Prioritize teams with prior exits" vs "No preference"

Backend effect: These preferences become the **UserPreference Model** that ranks Search (Sec 11), For You (Sec 10), Alpha Feed (Sec 16), and Emerging Radar (Sec 17).

## **45.1 Alpha Feed Defaults (Sec 16)**

## **45.2 Emerging Radar Defaults (Sec 17)**

## **45.3 Signal Tuning (Sec 14 + 15)**

Advanced controls to implement the 7-factor assessment in the user's own terms:

* **Signal Type Weights:** Development / Ecosystem / Social / Funding / On-chain / Market — sliders (Ignore → Important → Critical)
* **Sensitivity:** Conservative (fewer, stronger signals) ↔ Aggressive (more, earlier signals)
* **Signal Convergence Requirement:** Single strong signal OK vs. Require 2+ / 3+ converging signals (default: 2+ per Sec 6.3)
* **Counter-Signal Tolerance:** Always show counter-signals (default ON) / Hide weak counter-signals
* **Early-Stage Bias:** Prefer early-but-risky vs. Prefer validated traction
* **Hype Filter:** Strict (hide high-hype / low-evidence) / Balanced / Off

Each signal card must link: "Why this score? → View weights → Adjust"

## **45.4 Alerts & Monitoring (Sec 25)**

Per-watchlist-item overrides + global defaults:

* Alert types ON/OFF: Major dev, Ecosystem change, Narrative shift, Funding, Social spike, Market move, New connection, Signal convergence
* Alert threshold: Only Strong Signal / Moderate+ / Any Emerging
* Channel: In-app / Email / Push / Telegram/Discord (P2)
* Quiet hours + Max alerts per day (to avoid noise, per Sec 25)
* Per-item example: "Track EigenLayer: Alert me only on Funding + Mainnet, Strong only"

## **45.5 Research Depth & Evidence (Sec 13 + 23)**

* Default research depth: Quick overview / Standard (Overview → Signals → Evidence → Counter) / Deep (all + Timeline + Deep Research)
* Fact vs. Interpretation display: Always separate (default ON per Sec 23), Evidence inline vs. collapsed
* Language / Reading level: Technical vs. Simplified summaries

# **47\. New User Stories, Acceptance Criteria & Prioritization Update**

## **47.1 New User Stories — Control Center**
